What this is. A reference 23s9p module (one of four in the Tesla Model Y 4680 pack) filled with BYD FC4680 LFP cells, and a fast thermal twin of it. Each cell is two thermal nodes (core and can), cooled by serpentine ribbons between the rows that run out from the manifold and back. Heat is ohmic (I²R, R = 9 mΩ per cell, the measured 10 s pulse resistance) plus V²/Risc in a shorted cell. Two NTCs sit on the cells at the coolant inlet and outlet. Parameters come from the paper 1 parameter set where available: heat capacity 281 J/K and core-to-can resistance 0.49 K/W. The cell resistance, the ribbon conductance (1 W/K per side) and the cell-to-cell conduction (0.02 W/K) are estimates. Linked to the 3-D cell twin. When you place a short, choose where it sits in the cell (radius, height), its type, footprint and duration. The hottest point inside the shorted cell is then computed from the unit-short response library of the 3-D model (superposition of the short's power history) on top of the cell's can temperature from this module model, with the footprint factor of the refined-patch study. The library assumes the short's heat stays in the shorted cell over the time shown. It shows what the module's sensors can and cannot see, and what the cell's interior does meanwhile.
